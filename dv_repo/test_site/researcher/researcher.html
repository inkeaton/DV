For Researchers

Dive deep into the structure and evolution of visualization research. Explore citation patterns, identify collaboration opportunities, track topic trends, and discover emerging research fronts.

Citation Dynamics

Citation patterns reveal the impact and reach of visualization research. Average citations peaked around 2010-2013, reflecting the time needed for papers to accumulate citations.

The dashed line shows top paper citations, which can be 5-10x higher than the average, indicating high variance in impact.

Recent papers (2019+) have lower counts due to citation lag, not declining quality.

Collaboration Landscape

The visualization research community is highly interconnected. Key hub researchers often collaborate across institutions and continents.

Node size represents publication count, while link thickness indicates collaboration frequency. Colors show geographic distribution.

Identifying potential collaborators can accelerate research and open new methodological perspectives.

Topic Evolution

Research topics rise and fall over 35 years. Scientific Visualization dominated early years, while Visual Analytics grew rapidly after 2004.

Machine Learning for Visualization shows exponential growth since 2015, now rivaling traditional areas.

Immersive Analytics is the newest growth area, reflecting VR/AR adoption in research settings.

Research Powerhouses

A handful of institutions produce disproportionate research output. University of Washington, Georgia Tech, and MIT lead in both papers and citations.

European institutions like ETH Zurich and University of Stuttgart show high citation-per-paper ratios, indicating strong impact.

Consider these institutions when seeking sabbatical hosts, collaboration partners, or PhD placement for students.

Emerging Frontiers

Research fronts can be mapped by maturity (x-axis) and growth rate (y-axis). Bubble size shows 2024 paper count.

LLMs for Visualization is the hottest emerging area with 85% growth. Explainable AI Vis and Accessibility are also rapidly growing.

Mature areas like Network Analysis and Geo Visualization offer stability but lower novelty potential.

Strategic Insights for Researchers

  1. Citation lag is real — Papers need 5-10 years to reach peak citation count.
  2. Network matters — Hub researchers collaborate widely; consider expanding your network.
  3. Topics shift — ML for Vis and Immersive Analytics are the current growth areas.
  4. Institution selection — Top institutions offer collaboration and visibility advantages.
  5. Emerging fronts — LLMs for Vis and Explainability are high-risk, high-reward areas.
