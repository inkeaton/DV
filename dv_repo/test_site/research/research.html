Core Research Areas

Information Visualization leads with 28% of papers, followed closely by Scientific Visualization and Visual Analytics.

These three tracks now form the core of the conference, each with distinct communities but increasing overlap.

Rising Rigor

User studies have become the gold standard. In 2024, 68% of papers include formal user evaluation — up from just 15% in 1995.

Papers without evaluation have dropped from 40% to just 5%, reflecting higher methodological standards.

Real-World Impact

Biology & Medicine remains the top application domain, but Cybersecurity is growing fastest at +42%.

Climate visualization is surging (+22%) as researchers tackle communicating climate change to diverse audiences.

Data Diversity

Tabular data dominates, but network and graph visualization has become the second most common focus.

New challenges around text and documents are growing as NLP and LLM-powered tools reshape how we explore unstructured data.

Open Science Revolution

82% of papers now share open-source code — a dramatic shift from just 8% in 2010.

The community has embraced reproducibility, though preregistration (12%) and replication studies (8%) remain rare.

Key Takeaways

  1. InfoVis, SciVis, and VA form a balanced trifecta of research focus
  2. User studies are now expected, not optional
  3. Healthcare and climate are driving applied visualization research
  4. Data complexity has grown exponentially — 80 dimensions is now normal
  5. Open science has transformed from exception to norm
