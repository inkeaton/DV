Publication Growth

IEEE VIS has grown from a single conference track to a major venue with three distinct tracks: InfoVis, SciVis, and VAST.

The stacked area chart shows how each track has contributed to the growing body of visualization research over 35 years.

Citation Impact

Most papers receive between 10-50 citations, but a small number of highly influential papers drive the field forward.

The top 1% of papers account for over 25% of all citations, showing the outsized impact of seminal works like D3.js.

Trending Topics

Keywords reveal what's hot in visualization research. Machine Learning and Deep Learning have exploded in recent years.

Meanwhile, traditional topics like Volume Rendering are declining as new challenges emerge around explainability and accessibility.

Getting Longer

Papers have grown from an average of 8 pages in 1990 to over 11 pages today.

This reflects increasing expectations for thorough evaluation, reproducibility details, and supplementary materials like code and data.

Collaboration is Key

The most common paper has 3-4 authors. Single-author papers are increasingly rare (only 4% of all publications).

International collaborations now account for 25% of all papers, reflecting the global nature of visualization research.

Key Takeaways

  1. Publication volume has tripled since the 1990s across all tracks
  2. A small number of seminal papers drive most of the citation impact
  3. ML/AI integration is the fastest-growing trend in visualization
  4. Papers are getting longer with more comprehensive evaluations
  5. Collaboration is essential — solo papers are nearly extinct
