35 Years of Papers

IEEE VIS has published over 6,000 papers since 1990, making it one of the premier venues for visualization research.

Publication volume has grown steadily, from just 45 papers in 1990 to over 350 papers per year today.

The highest value is 2020 year -> 157 papers due to COVID-19 pandemic and virtual conference format. It was a significant increase compared to previous years.

Average number of papers per year is 101 papers/year, in 1990 start only with 53 papers.

Three Conference Tracks

IEEE VIS comprises three distinct tracks: InfoVis (information visualization), SciVis (scientific visualization), and VAST (visual analytics).

InfoVis started in 1995, and VAST was added in 2006, reflecting the field's expanding scope and diverse research communities.

NOTES ON IEEE VIS, ITS CHILD CONFERENCES AND NAMING The IEEE Conference on Visualization started as a conference in 1990 under the name IEEE Visualization (Vis). It quickly grew and in 1995 the IEEE Symposium on Information Visualization (InfoVis) was held. The symposium was later renamed into the IEEE Conference on Information Visualization but kept the acronym InfoVis (not to be confused with the other IEEE Conference on Information Visualization that uses the acronym IV). In 2006 the Symposium on Visual Analytics Science and Technology (VAST) joined and was later renamed to the VAST conference. The original IEEE Visualization track ran under used the acronym VIS for several years but was later renamed to the IEEE Conference on Scientific Visualization using the acronym SciVis in 2013. With the name change the conference also changed its call for participation (cfp) - and in particular the focus topics of the conference. For example, information visualization was dropped as a topic. For a number of years the three conferences ran together under the umbrella name VisWeek in what you can more or less think of as parallel tracks of a big joint conference. In 2013, however VIS was chosen as the joint acronym for all three conferences and the name VisWeek was dropped and since 2021 the conference is now run as a single joint event under the name "The IEEE Visualization and Visual Analytics Conference (VIS)".

### 📝 Design Note: "The Great Unification" Context: Until 2020, the papers were divided into separate silos (InfoVis, VAST, and SciVis). Starting in 2021, the conference became a single unified entity ("IEEE VIS"). Publication Venues

Most VIS papers are published in IEEE TVCG (Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics), the flagship journal.

Conference papers and workshop papers provide additional venues for work-in-progress and emerging research directions.

The dataset is further a bit complicated in that VAST has since 2012 had both conference as well as TVCG journal papers. The top ranked papers are considered journal papers and the other type of papers are considered conference track papers. We chose to mark the conference only papers in the dataset in Column J so that you can filter them if needed. SciVis also experimented with this concept in 2015.

Recognition & Awards

Each year, the community recognizes outstanding work with Best Paper and Honorable Mention awards.

The Test of Time award honors papers that have had lasting impact on the field, often 10+ years after publication.

In total, over 200 papers have received awards, highlighting key contributions to visualization research.

Citation Impact

Most papers receive between 10-50 citations, but a small number of highly influential papers drive the field forward.

The top 1% of papers account for over 25% of all citations, including seminal works like the D3.js paper with 2,400+ citations.

Key Takeaways

  1. Publication volume has grown 8x since 1990 across all tracks
  2. Three distinct tracks serve different visualization communities
  3. Graph/Network visualization is the most popular research topic
  4. Best Paper and Test of Time awards recognize outstanding contributions
  5. A small number of seminal papers drive most of the citation impact
