35 Years of Papers

IEEE VIS has published over 3,500 papers since 1990, making it one of the premier venues for visualization research.

Publication volume has grown steadily, from just 53 papers in 1990 to an average of around a hundred papers per year today.

Amongst all years, we see 2020 stand out: Well over 150 papers were published in that year. This was probably caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, causing authors to spend more time working at home and initiating a shift to a virtual conference format, which allows for more submissions and acceptances.

Different Banners,
Same Heart

Over the years, the IEEE VIS conference was divided into different tracks.

The IEEE Symposium on Information Visualization (InfoVis) was first held in 1995, with its focus on information visualization, in the general sense. In 2006, the Symposium on Visual Analytics Science and Technology (VAST) joined, focusing on visual analytics. The original IEEE Visualization track was renamed to the IEEE Conference on Scientific Visualization (SciVis) in 2013, with a focus on scientific visualization. During this period, the three conferences ran together under the umbrella name VisWeek in what can be thought of as parallel tracks of a big joint conference.

Lastly, in 2021, the three conferences were unified under the single banner of "The IEEE Visualization and Visual Analytics Conference (VIS)".

Changing Framings

We can see that at the beginning, most papers were published as conference papers, as part of the main conference tracks. Often these papers were also published in workshop proceedings.

The situation starts to shift in 2006, after the start of the VAST conference, since it also hosted journal papers, usually published in IEEE TVCG (Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics), the flagship journal. The top ranked papers were considered journal papers and the others conference track papers.

From that point on, we see a steady increase in journal publications, becoming the dominant publication type after 2020.

What Do We Visualize?

Data Visualization is an essential research area for any scientific discipline. As such, its papers spans a wide range of topics.

The treemap here highlights the most discussed ones, grouped by macro-category. Volume Rendering dominates the field, closely followed by Graph/Network visualization and Visual Programming.

An Award Winning Subject

In total, over 250 papers of the ones in our set have received awards, with 5 of them receiving multiple. This highlights their key contributions to visualization research.

Among them, The papers received different types of awards, including Best Paper, Best Application Paper, Honorable Mention and the Best Case Study award.

The Test of Time award honors papers that have had lasting impact on the field, often 10+ years after publication.

Papers highlighted with a green border have also received the Graphics Replicability Stamp, which certifies the reproducibility of their results.

Cite Me on That!

Most papers receive between 10-50 citations, but a small number of highly influential papers drive the field forward.

The top 1% of papers account for over 20% of all citations, including seminal works like the D3.js paper with almost four thousand citations.

Key Takeaways

  1. Publication volume has grown 8x since 1990 across all tracks
  2. Three distinct tracks serve different visualization communities
  3. Graph/Network visualization is the most popular research topic
  4. Best Paper and Test of Time awards recognize outstanding contributions
  5. A small number of seminal papers drive most of the citation impact
