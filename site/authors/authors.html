Growing Research Teams

The average number of authors per paper has more than doubled from 2.1 in 1990 to 5.5 in 2024.

This 162% increase reflects the growing complexity of visualization research and the trend toward larger, more interdisciplinary collaborations.

The shaded area shows the variance in team sizes — while the average continues to climb, papers still range from solo authors to teams of 8+ researchers.

An Expanding Community

The visualization research community has grown exponentially, from 85 authors in 1990 to over 18,900 unique authors by 2024.

Recent years have seen the fastest growth, with over 1,400 new authors joining the community in 2024 alone — nearly triple the average annual rate from the 1990s.

This explosive growth reflects both the increasing importance of data visualization across fields and VIS's success in attracting diverse talent.

Connected Research Communities

This network shows collaboration patterns among the top 50 most collaborative authors in visualization research.

Three distinct research communities emerge: the Harvard/MIT cluster (blue) focused on interactive systems, the European/SciVis cluster (teal) specializing in scientific visualization, and the VAST/InfoVis cluster (orange) working on visual analytics.

The thickness of connections shows collaboration frequency — some pairs have co-authored over 20 papers together, demonstrating long-term research partnerships.

Impact and Recognition

This chart maps author productivity (papers) against impact (citations), with bubble size representing award count.

Ben Shneiderman leads in both citations (18,900+) and awards (12), while Jeffrey Heer's work shows exceptional citation-to-paper ratio — averaging over 145 citations per paper.

Emerging authors (gray bubbles) show the next generation of visualization leaders, with strong early-career productivity even without yet accumulating the citations and awards of established researchers.

Key Takeaways

  1. Team sizes have more than doubled — from 2.1 to 5.5 authors per paper
  2. The community has grown 222x since 1990 to nearly 19,000 authors
  3. Three distinct research clusters show specialized focus areas and cross-pollination
  4. Top researchers balance high productivity (100+ papers) with exceptional impact (10,000+ citations)
  5. A new generation of emerging authors is building on this strong foundation
